# Cultural Contribution, Sustenance, and Preservation of the Cultural Library

> **LLM text | Human AI** · Human-origin social-system proposal, LLM-written reconstruction  
> **Provenance:** Author-prompt sketch (October 2026). Original detailed formulation unavailable.

## Original proposal in brief

The author described a system in which people receive **sustenance per unit of cultural contribution**, so that a **cultural library** can be preserved.

The proposed link is between the everyday material cost of sustaining people and the long-term collective value of their contributions to cultural memory. A civilization expends resources to keep individuals alive, while those individuals may maintain or expand the shared repertoire of knowledge, practices, stories, skills, and art.

## Unresolved design choices

A usable model would need to specify:

1. **Cultural contribution:** original creation, transmission, caretaking, teaching, translation, preservation, or all of these?
2. **Units of value:** who defines the unit and what stops the metric being gamed?
3. **The library:** a literal archive, a distributed social memory, or a broader metaphor?
4. **Allocation:** how are contributions recognized before or after their eventual cultural importance becomes known?
5. **Fairness:** how are quiet, less visible, or non-recorded forms of contribution treated?

## Critical ethical constraint

A person's access to essential food, water, and shelter should **not be made conditional on being judged culturally productive**. Such a rule would put disabled people, caregivers, the very young and old, and people whose contributions cannot be measured at risk. Any future version of this thought experiment should carefully distinguish **unconditional basic needs** from optional incentives, recognition, or preservation funding.

The above safeguard is an **editorial ethical concern**, not a claim that the author has already endorsed this design.

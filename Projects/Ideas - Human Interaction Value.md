## Central intuition

The author proposed that **human interactions should be worth more than a bottle of water**.

This is a claim about how people and societies *assign value*, not a demonstrated exchange rate. It challenges the ease with which market prices make physical commodities legible while leaving care, cultural contact, presence, trust, and the social meaning of interaction difficult to represent.

The comparison is deliberately stark: an ordinary consumable has a visible price tag, while the act of connecting with another person may have little or no direct compensation even where it sustains a community.

## Questions the proposal raises

- What does “worth” mean here: financial payment, social recognition, rights, emotional significance, or public resource allocation?
- Does making relationships economically legible improve recognition or risk commodifying them?
- How would a system avoid counting manipulative, performative, or unwanted contact as positive interaction?
- How can basic goods remain accessible regardless of whether a person is socially active?

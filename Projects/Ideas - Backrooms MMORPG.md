# Backrooms MMORPG: Hallucination, Memory, and Mapping

> **LLM text | Human AI** · Original game-system concept by the author, structured by LLM  
> **Provenance:** Recovered-detail summary from September 2025 design discussions. Design only; no implementation claimed.

## High concept

A **3D exploratory Backrooms MMORPG** combines unsettling manual exploration with a practical **idle/autopilot farming layer**. The player can look through a diegetic **2D Bomberman-like monitor** representing travel, mapped cells, and equipment-assisted information, while remaining vulnerable to events that require attention.

The world is unstable not just visually but spatially. Memory, maps, and collective investment determine what stays real.

## World generation

- Procedurally arranged, sealed chunks connect through doors, vents, portals and other transitions.
- Areas may rotate or vary in shape and gravity.
- Regions that are not remembered or otherwise anchored may change or regenerate.
- **Hallucinations** disturb perception, including the apparent existence, texture or character of environmental details.
- Sanity and memory mechanics modulate how confidently the player can rely on what appears on screen.

## Cartography as an economy

Mapping is more than filling a minimap. **Map items** can force a remembered region around the player, described in one earlier iteration as having roughly a **10 km radius**, and slow the local instability. Stability fades toward the edges of the mapped region.

Human and non-human classes may create or trade these maps. Hybrid mappers can accumulate mapping progress through distance travelled, producing expensive maps valuable to guilds or major groups. Linear or well-documented levels are easier and cheaper to map alone.

Persistent guild bases provide larger, more durable mapping/stability effects as participants collectively add travel and cartographic capability. Explorers therefore provide real value to established communities.

## People, creatures, and classes

- Humans, non-human playable races and hybrids have different capabilities and ways to participate economically.
- Class changes may require thresholds involving level, distance travelled, or insanity/sanity progression.
- Creatures/entities optimize **general pathing**, can encounter and predate one another, but **do not learn a specific human player's patterns**. Pattern-learning is reserved for people, non-human player classes, and certain level-host NPCs.
- Equipment may include backpacks, map/detection upgrades and other devices that make exploration or safe automation possible.

## Progression and idle play

Automation is intended as a **quality-of-life layer for already explored, lower-risk content**. Dangerous or poorly understood levels remain unfarmable until sufficient equipment and expertise exist. Manual intervention matters when the world becomes unstable or hostile.

An emergent long-term objective is to organize groups resembling **MEG**: stabilize Level 1, secure bases, and push collective mapping into unknown levels.

## Design questions (editorial)

- How much world instability is thrilling before mapmaking feels futile?
- How are hallucinations legible without rendering play unfair or inaccessible?
- How do persistent memory fields scale in a shared world?
- How do map creators earn sustained value without monopolizing exploration?
- What server architecture could maintain reproducible region generation?

# A Catalogue of Problems and Solutions to Non-Problems

> **LLM text | Human AI** · Platform concept proposed by the author, organized by LLM  
> **Provenance:** Recovered from October 2026 brainstorming.

## Two-column premise

The author suggested a public or licensed catalogue with two conspicuously different columns:

1. **Problems noticed in the world.**
2. **Solutions searching for a problem:** elaborate attempts to solve things that may not actually matter.

AI agents would research, analyze, and formalize catalogue entries. Over time the catalogue could become a marketplace where contributors submit entries and receive some share of value when other users buy rights or licenses to them. The author compared the longer-term contributor ecosystem loosely with *Roblox*: independent users make reusable things and participate in a market.

## What makes it interesting

The cataloguing act itself separates **problem discovery** from **solution enthusiasm**. A sophisticated implementation can be worthless if it serves no real need; conversely, a clear diagnosis of an overlooked problem can be useful before any solution exists.

## Open operational questions (editorial)

- Can ideas be licensed in the way users imagine, given that ideas themselves are often not exclusive intellectual property?
- How are plagiarism, provenance, and originality disputes resolved?
- Who verifies that a problem is real and affected users consent to any data use?
- What prevents low-quality AI-generated formalizations from flooding the catalogue?
- How does a creator get paid: subscriptions, research commissions, bounties, or specific licensed assets?

**Status:** Platform/monetization concept, not a validated business plan or existing company.
